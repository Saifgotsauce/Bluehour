# Claims matrix

Every health or product statement Blue Hour makes, the exact wording allowed, and what backs it (report section 9).
Ads, Crew scripts, captions, emails and pages all use this wording. Anything not here needs adding (with a source)
before it's used. Not legal advice: have a US supplement regulatory reviewer check this before scaling.

## Rules

- Structure/function wording only ("helps support normal ..."), with the asterisk and the FDA disclaimer in the footer.
- Never: hangover, cure, prevent, treat, detox, liver, recovery from drinking or drugs, "go harder", less sleep.
  The FDA's July 2020 warning letters went to 7 companies for hangover claims on supplements, and named NAC.
- Never put Prime (NAC) next to anything about drinking, mornings after, or "the night before".
- Ingredient research is about the ingredient, never "Blue Hour is proven to". Nobody has tested the pouch itself.
- Lab reports prove what's in a capsule, not what it does.
- The crossed-out $129 next to $99 stays (founder's decision). It must become the real price after the first 100 orders. If the plan ever changes to keep $99, take the $129 down.
