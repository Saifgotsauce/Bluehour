NAC is one of the strangest ingredients on a supplement shelf. It's sold next to vitamin C and zinc, but it's also a hospital drug, and a few years ago the FDA said it didn't legally count as a supplement at all.

Here's what NAC actually is, what's known about it and who should be careful with it.

**The short version:** NAC (N-acetylcysteine) is a form of the amino acid cysteine. Your body uses cysteine to make glutathione, one of its main antioxidants. Doctors have used NAC as a medicine since the 1960s. As a supplement it's generally well tolerated, but it can upset your stomach, it isn't absorbed very well when swallowed, and some people, including those taking nitroglycerin, should check with a doctor first.

## What NAC is

NAC is cysteine with an extra chemical group attached (an "acetyl" group). Cysteine is an amino acid your body can make itself and also gets from protein-rich food.

Your body uses cysteine to build **glutathione**, an antioxidant found in nearly every cell. Cysteine is usually the ingredient in shortest supply when your body makes glutathione, which is why NAC gets so much attention. A 2021 review in *Antioxidants* describes NAC's main known role as helping restore glutathione.

## NAC as a medicine

NAC was a drug long before it was a supplement.

- **Breaking up mucus.** NAC has been used since the 1960s to thin mucus so it can be coughed up. It's still given this way, often inhaled.
- **Acetaminophen (paracetamol) overdose.** Since the 1980s, NAC has been the standard antidote for acetaminophen overdose, given in hospital at doses far higher than any supplement.

The World Health Organization lists it as an essential medicine.

## Why the FDA almost pulled NAC from supplement shelves

US law says an ingredient can't be sold as a dietary supplement if it was first approved as a drug. NAC was approved as a drug in **1963**, years before it showed up in supplements.

For decades nobody enforced that. Then the FDA changed course:

- **2020:** The FDA sent warning letters stating that NAC was excluded from the definition of a dietary supplement because of its earlier approval as a drug.
- **March 2022:** The FDA denied petitions from two supplement industry groups, the Council for Responsible Nutrition and the Natural Products Association, confirming that NAC is excluded. It said it was still considering the industry's other request to make NAC lawful in supplements through a regulation.
- **August 2022:** The FDA issued final guidance saying it intends to use "enforcement discretion". In plain English, it won't act against NAC supplements that are otherwise legal while it reviews the safety data and decides on a rule.

That's why you can still buy NAC as a supplement in the US. The guidance says the FDA will let the industry know if that policy changes.

## How well is NAC absorbed?

Not very well. When you swallow NAC, only about **6 to 10%** reaches your bloodstream unchanged, according to the 2021 *Antioxidants* review. Much of it is processed in your gut and liver first.

That doesn't mean it does nothing. But be wary of any brand that talks about NAC as if every milligram goes straight to work.

## What the research does and doesn't show

You'll find hundreds of studies on NAC, many testing it as a treatment for specific medical conditions. Those are drug studies, mostly at medical doses and under a doctor's care, and the results are mixed. They don't tell you what a supplement will do for a healthy person.

As a supplement, what can honestly be said is that NAC provides cysteine, which your body uses to make glutathione. Be skeptical of anyone promising more than that.

## Side effects

Most people tolerate oral NAC well. The most common problems are:

- **Nausea, vomiting and stomach upset.** These are more common when NAC is swallowed than when it's given by drip.
- **Rash or itching.**
- **Unpleasant smell or taste.** NAC contains sulfur, and some capsules smell faintly of it.

Less common reactions include headache, low blood pressure and, rarely, tightness in the chest or wheezing. Severe allergy-like reactions have mostly been reported with high-dose NAC given by drip in hospital.

## Who should check with a doctor first

- **People with asthma.** Chest tightness and narrowing of the airways have been reported after NAC, especially when it's inhaled.
- **People taking nitroglycerin** for chest pain. Memorial Sloan Kettering notes that NAC can lower blood pressure further and cause severe headaches when combined with it.
- **People having cancer treatment.** It's still debated whether antioxidants like NAC can interfere with chemotherapy or radiation. Ask your oncologist.
- **Anyone pregnant or breastfeeding.**
- **Anyone with a stomach ulcer**, since NAC can irritate the stomach.

If you take any prescription medicine, a pharmacist can check NAC against your list in a couple of minutes.

## How much NAC do people take?

The US has no official upper limit for NAC, and no recommended daily amount, because it isn't an essential nutrient. Supplements commonly contain 600 mg per capsule.

More isn't automatically better, and stomach side effects become more likely as the dose goes up. If you take NAC, take it with food if it upsets your stomach.

## FAQ

### Is NAC a supplement or a drug?

Both. It was approved as a drug in 1963. In 2022 the FDA confirmed that, legally, this excludes it from the definition of a dietary supplement, but said it won't act against NAC supplements that are otherwise legal while it decides on a rule.

### What does NAC do in the body?

It provides cysteine, an amino acid your body uses to make the antioxidant glutathione. As a medicine, it's used to break up mucus and to treat acetaminophen overdose.

### Can NAC upset your stomach?

Yes. Nausea, vomiting and stomach upset are its most common side effects, and they're more common with tablets and capsules than with NAC given by drip.

### Who shouldn't take NAC?

Check with a doctor first if you have asthma or a stomach ulcer, take nitroglycerin, are having cancer treatment, or are pregnant or breastfeeding.

### Why does NAC smell like sulfur?

NAC contains sulfur. A faint smell is normal and doesn't mean the product has gone bad.
