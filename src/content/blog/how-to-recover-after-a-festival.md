Three days of sets, sun, dust, portaloos and four hours of sleep a night. The festival was worth it. Now you have to be a functioning adult again.

There's no magic fix for a festival weekend. What helps is getting the basics right in the right order: getting home safely, sleeping properly, eating and drinking sensibly, and looking after the things that took a hit, like your ears. Here's the plan.

## Step 1: Get home safely

The drive home is often the riskiest part of the whole weekend, and it's usually after the least sleep.

According to the CDC's workplace safety institute, being awake for **17 hours** affects you about as much as a blood alcohol level of 0.05%, and **24 hours** awake about as much as 0.10%, which is above the US legal driving limit of 0.08%. Festival sleep in a tent is also short and broken, so you might be more impaired than the clock suggests.

- **Pick the driver before you leave home**, and make sure they get a proper night's sleep before the drive.
- **Don't leave straight after the last set** if you're driving. Sleep first, even if it means leaving later.
- **Know the warning signs:** yawning nonstop, heavy eyelids, drifting out of your lane, missing exits or not remembering the last few miles. If you notice any of them, pull over somewhere safe. The CDC suggests a coffee and a 15 to 30 minute nap, but that's only a short-term fix.

If nobody is fit to drive, take the shuttle, the train or a bus, or stay another night.

## Step 2: The first night back

You'll probably want to crash the moment you get home. That's fine, but set yourself up for it:

- **Shower and cool down.** Get the dust, sunscreen and sweat off. A cooler body falls asleep more easily.
- **Eat something real.** A proper meal, not another round of festival fries.
- **Drink water, but don't chug.** Sip steadily. Drinking huge amounts very fast can dilute the salt in your blood, which is dangerous. Our guide to [staying hydrated at a festival](/blog/how-to-stay-hydrated-at-a-festival) explains how much is enough and when electrolytes help.
- **Skip the "recovery" beers.** Alcohol helps you fall asleep but breaks up the second half of the night and cuts down REM sleep, so you wake up less rested.
- **Go to bed, phone away.** Our guide to [why you can't sleep after a concert](/blog/cant-sleep-after-a-concert) covers what to do if you're exhausted but wired.

## Step 3: Get your sleep back on track

A festival is basically a few nights of short sleep followed by jet lag without the flight. You need two things: enough sleep, and your body clock back where it belongs.

**Get enough.** The American Academy of Sleep Medicine recommends that adults get **7 or more hours** a night. After a festival, give yourself a few nights with an earlier bedtime rather than one 14-hour sleep.

**Don't count on one big lie-in to fix it.** In a 2019 University of Colorado Boulder study, people who slept in on the weekend after a week of short sleep only got about an hour of extra sleep on average, and the benefits disappeared when short sleep resumed.

**Protect your wake-up time.** Sleeping until mid-afternoon pushes your body clock later and makes Monday night harder. Get up at a sensible time, get daylight early, and keep any nap short and before mid-afternoon.

Our guide to [resetting your sleep after a weekend out](/blog/reset-sleep-after-a-weekend-out) walks through it hour by hour.

## Step 4: Feed yourself properly

After days of festival food, your body will appreciate the boring stuff:

- **Proper meals** with protein, vegetables and some carbs
- **Fruit and vegetables** to make up for three days of not much of either
- **Salty food and fluids together** if you sweated a lot. Soup is great for this.

There's no special "detox" needed. Your liver and kidneys handle that. Regular meals, water and sleep do the work.

## Step 5: Check your ears

If your ears are still ringing or sound muffled, that's a sign they've had more than they could comfortably take.

- Give them a rest: no headphones on high volume, no loud bars for a few days.
- Ringing usually fades within a day or two.
- **If it lasts more than a couple of days, or your hearing still sounds muffled, see a doctor or an audiologist.**

Next time, wear earplugs. Our guide to [protecting your hearing at raves and festivals](/blog/protect-your-hearing-at-raves-and-festivals) covers which ones keep the music sounding good.

## Why so many people get sick after festivals

"Festival flu" is real in the sense that festivals are good at spreading germs. Thousands of people share air, drinks, food stalls and not enough handwashing stations, and most of them are short on sleep.

- A 2013 review in *Eurosurveillance* looked at outbreaks linked to large open-air festivals and found both respiratory infections and stomach bugs like norovirus. It linked them to poor hygiene, inadequate sanitation and gaps in vaccination.
- A 2025 review led by Flinders University of 19 multi-day events attended mostly by young people found that infectious disease outbreaks and foodborne illness were the most common public health threats. Foodborne illnesses such as hepatitis A made up more than one in five incidents.

What helps:

- **Wash your hands or use hand sanitizer**, especially before eating and after the toilets
- **Don't share drinks, vapes or bottles**
- **Ask your doctor whether your vaccinations are up to date** before a big festival season, including flu
- **If you get sick afterwards, rest and stay away from others** while you're contagious

**See a doctor** if you have a high fever, can't keep fluids down, have bloody diarrhea, or feel confused or very weak.

## Step 6: Ease back into the week

- **Plan a light Monday** if you can. Don't schedule your hardest work for the day after you get back.
- **Unpack and wash everything** before it turns into a science experiment.
- **Move a little.** A walk outside gets you daylight, which helps reset your body clock.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to recover from a festival?

Most people feel mostly back to normal within a few days, as long as they sleep properly and keep their wake-up time steady. Ringing ears should fade within a day or two.

### Why do I always get sick after a festival?

Festivals put thousands of people close together with shared food, drinks and limited handwashing, while everyone is short on sleep. Respiratory infections and stomach bugs both spread easily in those conditions.

### Should I sleep all day after a festival?

A long sleep the first night is fine, but don't sleep until mid-afternoon every day. Get up at a reasonable time, get daylight and go to bed a bit earlier for a few nights.

### Is it safe to drive home from a festival?

Only if the driver has had a proper sleep. Being awake for 17 hours affects driving about as much as a 0.05% blood alcohol level, and 24 hours about as much as 0.10%.

### What should I eat after a festival?

Regular, balanced meals with protein, vegetables, fruit and some carbs, plus water. No special detox is needed.
